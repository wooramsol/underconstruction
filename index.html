Let there be light

I found a way to save on my electric bill. The bulb is off. The light is pure code. A WebGL shader paints it in real time, while MediaPipe finds your hands and body so the glow lands where it should. I first tried training a model on the bulb, but clear glass is really hard to track, so I stuck a green sticker on it instead. Any ideas for detecting a clear bulb in the dark? Make Me Cube Master

Have you heard of God‘s Number? Every 3×3 Rubik’s Cube can be solved in 20 moves or fewer. It was mathematically proven in 2010. Finding that shortest solution yourself is nearly impossible. But computers can. I built a tool that lets you follow the shortest solution, step by step. 1. Colour calibration: Scan all 6 sticker colours for your lighting 2. Scanning: Capture all 6 faces of your scrambled cube with the front camera (If a colour looks wrong, tap that face in the bar to re-scan) 3. Computing: The solver finds a sequence within 20 moves 4. Solution: Follow the 3D guide Demo: https://www.youtube.com/shorts/P-S4AqNKu9Q

BlinkType

What if you could "talk" using only your eyes? So I built BlinkType. How it works: - Webcam + MediaPipe Face Landmarker tracks your face - Short blink → dot (·), long blink → dash (−) - Pause briefly → the Morse sequence becomes a letter - Shake your head left/right → backspace Demo: https://www.youtube.com/shorts/a3krPiVVfKI

Butane Canister Recycling Tool

Butane gas canisters are among the most common types of gas containers, but recycling them is often inconvenient—and sometimes even dangerous. To recycle them safely, a hole must be punctured to release pressure and any remaining gas, making the metal safe for disposal. Most people need to carry a separate tool for this, but even that small inconvenience can discourage proper recycling. This concept proposes a simple solution: a built-in metal clip in the canister itself. Inexpensive to manufacture and easy to use, the clip punctures the can when struck against a flat surface.

Breathfrom

A container for capturing human breath. This balloon can be inflated and kept as a keepsake or given as a gift to commemorate a special moment. BREATH offers a unique way to preserve fleeting memories. Made entirely from thermoplastic polyurethane, it holds up to 500 millilitres of air, which is roughly the average volume exhaled in a single breath by an adult. Users can write directly on the balloon to note whose breath it contains and the date it was exhaled, preserving it for posterity. Crowdfunding attempt on Kickstarter was unsuccessful.
